# AUTH-0094: *On Davis v. The Gap, Inc.*

## Citation and source

- 246 F.3d 152 (2d Cir. 2001). [Opinion reproduction](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/246/246.F3d.152.99-9081.html); [published-opinion mirror](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/246/152/469017/).
- Later history/current status: no overruling authority located in this verification pass.

## Procedural posture and weight

Published Second Circuit merits decision; binding in that circuit and persuasive elsewhere.

## Claims and requested relief

A jewelry designer alleged unauthorized use of eyeglass designs in advertising and sought copyright actual damages, including the fair market value of a license.

## Holdings and relief actually awarded

The court held that actual damages may include the reasonable value of the use of a copyrighted work, including a hypothetical license fee, when supported by non-speculative proof; it reversed the relevant summary judgment disposition.

## What it supports

- Commercial-license evidence can support a valuation theory when comparable and proved.
- A hypothetical fee is compensatory evidence, not compelled assent to a future license.

## What it does not establish

- That a list price, settlement demand, or dual-license price is automatically recoverable.
- Profits, fees, statutory damages, or a GPL/AGPL remedy.

## Key facts and reasoning

The court distinguished legally cognizable fair-market value from speculation and required a factual basis for the proposed fee.
