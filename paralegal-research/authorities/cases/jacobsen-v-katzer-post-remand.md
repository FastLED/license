## Procedural posture and weight

This card supplements, rather than replaces, the Federal Circuit remand card
AUTH-0001. The December 2009 order decided cross-motions for summary judgment;
the February 2010 order then dismissed the case. The district-court rulings are
persuasive only and the dismissal is not a final contested merits award.

| Date | Posture | Actual result |
|---|---|---|
| 2009-12-10 | Summary judgment | Plaintiff's motion granted in part and denied in part; defendants' partial-summary-judgment motion denied. |
| 2010-02-22 | Dismissal | Order of dismissal; no public settlement terms or damages/injunction judgment located. |

## Claims and requested relief

Following the Federal Circuit remand, the cross-motions addressed copyright
infringement, DMCA copyright-management information, cybersquatting, and
counterclaims. The cited 2009 order did not itself award the preliminary
injunction or public source disclosure sometimes associated with broad accounts
of the litigation.

## Holdings and relief actually awarded

- The court ruled that the selection and arrangement of the decoder-definition
  files could satisfy the minimal originality threshold; it denied defendants'
  summary-judgment attack on copyrightability.
- It granted Jacobsen summary judgment on copyright infringement of the defined
  files and on cybersquatting, while recognizing remaining DMCA knowledge/intent
  issues. It did not set damages in the cited order.
- The later dismissal ends the docket without an accessible public final order
  granting damages, a permanent injunction, public source disclosure, or a
  settlement term. Do not convert the 2009 liability rulings into such relief.

## What it supports

- The leading 2008 appellate condition analysis was followed by fact- and
  work-specific district-court rulings; it did not itself produce a published
  source-performance remedy.
- A condition/licence case can also require independent proof of ownership,
  protected expression, copying, damages, and equitable relief.

## What it does not establish

- Any GPL/AGPL construction or a general source-disclosure entitlement.
- The complete terms or consideration of a settlement.
- A final damages award following the 2009 summary-judgment ruling.

## Key facts and reasoning

The post-remand order addressed JMRI decoder-definition files and attribution
information, not an obligation to publish a derivative work's source. Its
copyright discussion focuses on the selection/arrangement and files proved in
the record. The docket dismissal therefore materially limits any assertion that
the Federal Circuit remand culminated in a final open-source remedy.
