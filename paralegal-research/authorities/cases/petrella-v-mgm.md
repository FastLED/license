# AUTH-0097: *Petrella v. Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer, Inc.*

## Citation and source

- 572 U.S. 663 (2014). [Official U.S. Reports volume](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/572bv.pdf).
- Later history/current status: later monetary-lookback issue addressed in AUTH-0098; current precedent checked 2026-08-25.

## Procedural posture and weight

Merits decision, binding nationwide.

## Claims and requested relief

Copyright owner alleged continuing exploitation of *Raging Bull* and sought damages and injunction. Defendant invoked laches.

## Holdings and relief actually awarded

The Court held that laches cannot bar a claim for damages brought within the three-year limitations period in section 507(b). It described the ordinary separate-accrual rule for successive infringements and remanded; equitable delay may affect equitable relief in appropriate circumstances.

## What it supports

- Repeated acts ordinarily require separate dates and can create separately accruing claims.
- Delay is not a categorical bar to timely legal relief under section 507(b).

## What it does not establish

- Whether the discovery rule governs copyright accrual.
- That every continuing source/noncompliance allegation is a new infringement act.

## Key facts and reasoning

The Court applied the statute's limitations period and distinguished legal damages from equitable relief.
