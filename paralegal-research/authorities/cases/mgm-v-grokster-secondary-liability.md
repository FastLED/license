# AUTH-0114: *Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer Studios Inc. v. Grokster Ltd.*

## Citation and source

- 545 U.S. 913 (2005).
- [Opinion reproduction](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/pdf/04-480P.ZO); [opinion text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/04-480).
- Later history/current status: Supreme Court merits decision; checked 2026-08-25.
- Original language and translation provenance: English; not needed.

## Procedural posture and weight

Supreme Court merits decision. Its inducement rule binds U.S. courts on that
federal copyright proposition, while application depends on proof and does not
erase other secondary-liability limits.

## Claims and requested relief

Copyright holders sought damages and an injunction against distributors of
peer-to-peer software for users' alleged direct infringement. This was not a
public-license, reseller, or manufacturer case.

## Holdings and relief actually awarded

The Court held that one who distributes a device with the object of promoting
its use to infringe copyright, shown by clear expression or affirmative steps
to foster infringement, is liable for resulting third-party infringement. The
decision addressed the liability standard and remanded; it is not an order that
a distributor must publish source code or accept a license.

## What it supports

- Secondary liability is analytically distinct from direct infringement by the
  actor that reproduced, distributed, or otherwise exercised an exclusive right.
- Distribution or resale alone is not the complete inducement test; direct
  infringement and the required intent/conduct must be proved.

## What it does not establish

- Liability for a contract manufacturer, reseller, customer, or marketplace on
  a bare allegation that it handled a product containing code.
- A license-specific cure, source-availability, or affirmative-performance remedy.

## Key facts and reasoning

The Court preserved the line between distributing technology with lawful uses
and purposeful promotion of infringement. Supply-chain facts—who made which
copy, directed which distribution, knew what, and took what affirmative
steps—therefore matter before assigning a secondary theory.
