# AUTH-0102: *Friends of the Earth, Inc. v. Laidlaw Environmental Services (TOC), Inc.*

## Citation and source

- 528 U.S. 167 (2000). [Official U.S. Reports record](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USREPORTS-528/USREPORTS-528-167).
- Later history/current status: current Supreme Court precedent checked 2026-08-25.

## Procedural posture and weight

Merits decision, binding general Article III/mootness precedent.

## Claims and requested relief

Environmental organizations sought declaratory/injunctive relief and civil penalties for permit violations; the defendant later complied and asserted mootness.

## Holdings and relief actually awarded

The Court held that voluntary cessation of allegedly unlawful conduct ordinarily does not moot a case unless it is absolutely clear the conduct cannot reasonably be expected to recur. It also addressed standing and civil penalties in its statutory setting.

## What it supports

- Later compliance alone ordinarily does not end a live dispute over prospective or completed conduct.

## What it does not establish

- A copyright remedy, license breach, or automatic availability of an injunction after cure.
- That all damages claims survive any settlement, release, or lack of standing.

## Key facts and reasoning

The Court distinguished post-filing mootness from initial standing and applied the voluntary-cessation exception in an environmental enforcement case.
