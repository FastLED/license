# AUTH-0100: *Effects Associates, Inc. v. Cohen*

## Citation and source

- 908 F.2d 555 (9th Cir. 1990). [Opinion reproduction](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F2/908/908.F2d.555.88-6455.html).
- Later history/current status: no overruling authority located in this verification pass.

## Procedural posture and weight

Published Ninth Circuit merits decision; binding in that circuit and persuasive elsewhere.

## Claims and requested relief

Creator of film special-effects footage sued for copyright infringement; defendant asserted an implied nonexclusive license.

## Holdings and relief actually awarded

The court affirmed summary judgment based on an implied nonexclusive license arising from the parties' objective conduct and delivery for the intended film use.

## What it supports

- Authorization can be express or, on appropriate facts, implied; scope remains critical.

## What it does not establish

- That public licenses are implied, that a license survives breach, or that every delayed enforcement waives rights.

## Key facts and reasoning

The court considered the request to create the work, delivery, and intended use; it also distinguished nonexclusive permission from transfers requiring writing.
