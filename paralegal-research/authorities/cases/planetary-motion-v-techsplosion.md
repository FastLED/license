# AUTH-0030: *Planetary Motion, Inc. v. Techsplosion, Inc.*

## Citation and source
- 261 F.3d 1188 (11th Cir. 2001). [Published opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/261/1188/570628/).
- Later history/current status: None located in reviewed published record. Direct source is reporter reproduction; authenticate if wording becomes material.

## License, claims, requested and actual relief
- License/version and claims: GNU GPL (version not stated) governed Coolmail distribution, but no GPL claim was pleaded. Trademark/dilution and unfair-competition claims were litigated.
- Requested versus actual relief: Plaintiff requested trademark injunction, profits, damages, fees, and costs. District court entered trademark injunction and fees/costs; damages were denied for lack of specificity. The appellate court affirmed in material part and modified the injunction.

## Procedural posture and precedential weight
Published Eleventh Circuit trademark appeal; binding only for its trademark holdings.

## Holding
Internet distribution of GPL software could support trademark use/priority. It does not decide GPL enforceability, copyright infringement, source disclosure, or GPL remedies.

## Key facts and reasoning
Darrah released Coolmail free under GPL with modification notices; Planetary later acquired IP and sued over the mark.
