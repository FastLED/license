# AUTH-0060: *Jelurida IP B.V. v Apollo Fintech Ltd*

## Citation and source

- Full citation: Gerechtshof Amsterdam, 6 September 2022, ECLI:NL:GHAMS:2022:2582; first instance ECLI:NL:RBAMS:2020:4717.
- Direct primary source: [Dutch judgment text](https://uitspraken.rechtspraak.nl/details?id=ECLI:NL:GHAMS:2022:2582); accessible mirror with full disposition: [Lexboost](https://www.lexboost.com/rechtspraak/ECLI:NL:GHAMS:2022:2582).
- Later history: no later disposition located in this pass.

## Procedural posture and weight

Dutch Court of Appeal interim-relief judgment. Original language Dutch. No
official English translation located; all English wording is a limited
researcher translation/paraphrase. It has persuasive comparative value, not
binding effect outside its system.

## Claims, relief, and holding

- Licence/version: Jelurida Public License (JPL), after earlier Nxt GPL history.
- Claims: Jelurida alleged Apollo Currency used Nxt/Apollo software without JPL compliance.
- Requested versus awarded relief: the appellate court narrowed but affirmed the compliance order; it ordered Apollo, within the EU, fully and unconditionally to comply with JPL Articles 2.1, 2.2, 3 with 3.2, and 5. The Dutch penalty mechanism remained, reported as EUR25,000 per breach capped at EUR2.5m; costs were awarded.
- Holding: Apollo's attacks on JPL obligation (including alleged GPL/history defenses) failed on the interim record; order was limited to specified clauses.

## What it does not establish

- GPL enforcement, a final damages adjudication, or a worldwide order.
- Compliance with clauses beyond those expressly ordered.

## Key facts and reasoning

Apollo was a Hong Kong entity; the JPL selected Amsterdam. The court accepted
jurisdiction and restricted the performance command to defined JPL obligations,
an important distinction from an unlimited "obey the licence" injunction.
