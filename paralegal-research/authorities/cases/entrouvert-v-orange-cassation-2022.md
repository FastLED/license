# AUTH-0052: *Entr'Ouvert v Orange* — Court of Cassation

## Citation and source

- Full citation: Cass. 1re civ., 5 October 2022, No. 21-15.386, Arrêt No. 705 FS-B.
- Direct primary source: [official French judgment](https://www.courdecassation.fr/decision/633d28bda3bbc43e2e4d4b6a); [Court's English translation/page](https://www.courdecassation.fr/toutes-les-actualites/2022/10/05/clarification-admissibility-software-copyright-infringement-action).
- Later history: remanded to differently composed Paris Court of Appeal, which decided RG 22/18071 on 14 February 2024.

## Procedural posture and weight

French supreme-court partial quashing and remand. The French judgment controls;
the Court's own English page is the translation source used for its dispositive
language. It decides admissibility, not final GPL liability or a remedy amount.

## Claims, relief, and holding

- Licence/version: GNU GPL v2, Lasso.
- Claim/trigger: author alleged noncompliant use/distribution in Orange's IDMP platform.
- Requested versus awarded relief: no copyright damages/injunction awarded by this cassation ruling; it quashed only the appeal's inadmissibility finding, remanded, awarded costs, and ordered EUR5,000 under CPC Article 700.
- Holding: the copyright action could not be rejected on the stated non-cumulation ground where the alleged GPL breach was invoked to show unauthorized use; the relevant part of the 2021 appeal was quashed.

## What it does not establish

- That every GPL breach is infringement, that the GPL terms were breached here, or that source disclosure is a remedy.
- A final award of commercial-licence value.

## Key facts and reasoning

The Court described Lasso as available under a free licence or a paid commercial
licence. It separated the asserted copyright action from a blanket procedural
bar based merely on the licence contract.
