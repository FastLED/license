# AUTH-0053: *Entr'Ouvert v Orange* — Paris remand appeal

French appellate merits decision after cassation/remand. Original language
French. No official English translation was located; statements here are a
limited researcher translation/paraphrase, not a certified translation.

## Claims, relief, and holding

- Licence/version: GNU GPL v2, Lasso library.
- Claims: copyright infringement and associated harm from Orange/Orange Business Services' IDMP distribution to the State; GPL Articles 2, 3, 4, and 10 and moral-right allegations were pleaded.
- Requested relief: claimant sought, among other items, EUR3m economic loss, EUR500k moral harm, EUR500k profits, and publication measures.
- Awarded relief: EUR500,000 economic harm, EUR150,000 moral harm, EUR150,000 infringer profits, and EUR60,000 CPC Article 700 costs, plus first-instance/appellate costs including judicial-expertise costs; it rejected the requested publication measure. No affirmative source-publication order was awarded.
- Holding: on expert evidence, Lasso was modified and distributed within IDMP without the GPLv2-required terms/source offer; that unauthorized distribution was copyright infringement.

## What it does not establish

- An automatic rule that all combined/proprietary software must be released.
- That a judge compelled Orange to buy a commercial licence or publicly release source.
- Finality beyond the verified appellate record.

## Key facts and reasoning

The court found Lasso original, accepted Entr'Ouvert's ownership, and relied on
expert examination of modifications/distribution. Commercial proposals supplied
context for economic loss; they were not an imposed commercial contract.
