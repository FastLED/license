# AUTH-0091: *eBay Inc. v. MercExchange, L.L.C.*

## Citation and source

- 547 U.S. 388 (2006). [Official U.S. Reports volume](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/547bv.pdf).
- Later history/current status: current Supreme Court precedent checked 2026-08-25.

## Procedural posture and weight

Merits decision, binding federal equitable-remedy precedent.

## Claims and requested relief

Patent owner sought a permanent injunction after infringement. The case did not involve copyright, public licenses, source code, or specific performance.

## Holdings and relief actually awarded

The Court rejected categorical rules for or against injunctions and required application of the traditional four-factor test: irreparable injury, inadequate legal remedies, balance of hardships, and public interest. It vacated and remanded; it did not itself order an injunction.

## What it supports

- A permanent injunction for infringement is not automatic.
- A request to stop unlicensed conduct must be analyzed separately from a request for affirmative performance.

## What it does not establish

- Entitlement to a copyright injunction on a GPL/AGPL/MPL record.
- A right to public source disclosure, a commercial license, or Rule 70 enforcement.

## Key facts and reasoning

The Court applied ordinary equitable principles to patent injunctions and refused both categorical denial and categorical grant rules.
