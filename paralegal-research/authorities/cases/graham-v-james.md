# AUTH-0073: *Graham v. James*

## Citation and source

- 144 F.3d 229 (2d Cir. 1998), [published opinion reproduction](https://openjurist.org/144/f3d/229/graham-v-d-james).

## Procedural posture and weight

Published Second Circuit merits appeal. Binding in that circuit; it supplies a
general licensing distinction, not construction of a reciprocal public license.

## Holdings and relief actually awarded

The court held that a failure to pay royalties was ordinarily a covenant rather
than a condition precedent to a nonexclusive software license, so nonpayment
did not itself make licensed use infringement. It remanded for whether the
license was rescinded; it did not hold payment can never be a condition.

## What it supports

- The effect of breach follows the text and applicable contract law; a breached
  promise may leave permission intact unless it is a condition or the license
  is effectively terminated.

## What it does not establish

- That every source or notice provision is a covenant, or that a license's
  express condition/termination wording is ineffective.
