# AUTH-0098: *Warner Chappell Music, Inc. v. Nealy*

## Citation and source

- 601 U.S. 366 (2024). [Official opinion PDF](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/22-1078_4gci.pdf).
- Later history/current status: current Supreme Court precedent checked 2026-08-25.

## Procedural posture and weight

Merits decision, binding nationwide.

## Claims and requested relief

Copyright owner asserted that the discovery rule made its claim timely and sought damages for older infringements. The parties accepted the discovery-rule premise for the question presented.

## Holdings and relief actually awarded

The Court held that the Copyright Act supplies no separate three-year damages cap for a claim timely under the discovery rule. It expressly assumed, without deciding, that the discovery rule governs accrual.

## What it supports

- Do not impose an extra three-year monetary lookback if the claim is otherwise timely under an applicable discovery rule.

## What it does not establish

- That the discovery rule applies in every circuit or on every copyright claim.
- Timeliness of any license dispute or state-law claim.

## Key facts and reasoning

The Court read section 507(b) as a filing-period provision after accrual, not a separate damages limitation.
