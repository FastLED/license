# AUTH-0111: Federal Rules of Civil Procedure on discovery, ESI, protective orders, and sanctions

## Citation and source

- Fed. R. Civ. P. 1, 8, 11, 12, 16, 26, 34, 37, and 45.
- [Official Federal Rules of Civil Procedure (current PDF)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf); [Federal Judiciary rules page](https://www.uscourts.gov/forms-rules/current-rules-practice-procedure/federal-rules-civil-procedure).
- Later history/current status: the Federal Judiciary reports amendments through 2025; check the version in force when a matter is filed.
- Original language and translation provenance: English; not needed.

## Procedural posture and weight

Operative federal procedural rules in U.S. district courts, subject to statutes,
local rules, court orders, and controlling interpretation. The taxonomy's
`administrative-material` category is used because it has no separate `rule`
type; these are not merely guidance.

## Claims and requested relief

Rules 8 and 12 regulate pleadings and motions; Rule 26 sets scope, timing,
proportionality, ESI, privilege, and protective-order mechanisms; Rules 34 and
45 govern requests to parties and subpoenas to nonparties; Rule 37 addresses
discovery sanctions. They regulate process after a case begins; they do not
authorize private pre-suit acquisition of another person's data.

## Holdings and relief actually awarded

No particular order, discovery award, or sanction is established.

## What it supports

- Publicly obtainable materials and court-compelled production are distinct
  evidence channels.
- A protective order can govern discovery material, including source code, but
  is not a mechanism for public source release.
- ESI preservation, collection, production format, proportionality, privilege,
  and sanctions require case-specific analysis and court/local-rule practice.

## What it does not establish

- Entitlement to expedited discovery, a source-code inspection, an AEO tier,
  sanctions, or disclosure of confidential materials in a particular case.
- A duty to publish code publicly or a merits finding of infringement.

## Key facts and reasoning

Rule 26 distinguishes discovery's relevance/proportionality framework from the
separate merits and remedial questions. Rule 26(c) permits protective orders on
good cause; Rule 37(e) has a distinct ESI-loss framework. A party should
preserve authentic records without treating a contemplated discovery request as
permission to access systems or data it cannot lawfully access.
