# AUTH-0071: 17 U.S.C. §§ 201, 204, and 501

## Citation and source

- Copyright Act ownership, transfer-writing, and infringement-standing
  provisions; [official House Code Collection, § 204](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title17-section204&num=0&edition=prelim), with §§ 201 and 501 in the same title.

## Procedural posture and weight

Federal statute. It supplies the baseline for U.S. copyright ownership and
plaintiff status; state contract law still governs many assignment and
beneficiary issues.

## Holdings and relief actually awarded

Not applicable. Section 201 identifies initial ownership and divisible
exclusive rights; § 204(a) generally requires a signed writing for a transfer
of copyright ownership other than by operation of law; § 501(b) permits a
legal or beneficial owner of an exclusive right to sue for infringement of that
right while owner.

## What it supports

- Component-by-component ownership and transfer proof are required before
  treating a contributor, steward, or assignee as an infringement plaintiff.
- A nonexclusive license is not itself a transfer of copyright ownership.

## What it does not establish

- That a contributor owns an entire collective work, an assignee obtained an
  exclusive right, or a recipient is a third-party beneficiary.
