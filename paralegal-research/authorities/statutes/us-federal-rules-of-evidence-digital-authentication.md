# AUTH-0112: Federal Rules of Evidence for authentication and digital records

## Citation and source

- Fed. R. Evid. 901, 902(13)–(14), and 1001–1008.
- [Official Federal Rules of Evidence (current PDF)](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-evidence.pdf); [Federal Judiciary rules page](https://www.uscourts.gov/forms-rules/current-rules-practice-procedure/federal-rules-evidence).
- Later history/current status: current text checked 2026-08-25; source page identifies amendments through December 1, 2025.
- Original language and translation provenance: English; not needed.

## Procedural posture and weight

Operative evidence rules for most federal proceedings. State courts and foreign
courts may use different rules. The taxonomy's `administrative-material` label
does not reduce their legal force.

## Claims and requested relief

These rules address admissibility foundations, not infringement, source-code
ownership, or discovery. Rules 902(13) and (14) can allow specified certified
electronic-process and copied-data evidence to be self-authenticating with the
required certification and notice; Rule 901 still supplies the general
authentication rule.

## Holdings and relief actually awarded

No merits or evidentiary ruling is supplied by the rule text.

## What it supports

- Hashes, capture logs, preservation records, repository metadata, and witness
  evidence can be relevant to an authentication foundation, but each needs a
  documented process and applicable rule analysis.
- A public web page or code archive should be captured with its URL, time,
  method, and context; a hash alone proves neither authorship nor legal effect.

## What it does not establish

- That any particular screenshot, hash, repository tag, binary comparison, or
  automated capture is admissible or conclusive.
- Chain of title, license assent, source completeness, or infringement.

## Key facts and reasoning

The rules distinguish authenticity from weight and from merits. Preserve the
original available artifact and the repeatable acquisition record where lawful;
avoid altering timestamps, metadata, or file contents in the working copy.
