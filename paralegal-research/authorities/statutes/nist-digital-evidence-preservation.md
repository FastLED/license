# AUTH-0113: NISTIR 8387 digital-evidence preservation considerations

## Citation and source

- Barbara Guttman, Douglas R. White & Shannan Williams, *Digital Evidence Preservation: Considerations for Evidence Handlers*, NISTIR 8387 (Sept. 2022).
- [NIST publication record](https://www.nist.gov/publications/digital-evidence-preservation-considerations-evidence-handlers); [DOI-resolved full text](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.IR.8387).
- Later history/current status: publication record checked 2026-08-25.
- Original language and translation provenance: English; not needed.

## Procedural posture and weight

Official technical guidance, not a rule of evidence, statute, or judicial
holding. It can support an operational preservation checklist but cannot decide
admissibility or create a duty in a particular private dispute.

## Claims and requested relief

Not applicable. The report discusses integrity, storage, documentation,
hashing, digital signatures, and chain of custody in a digital-evidence
context.

## Holdings and relief actually awarded

None.

## What it supports

- Document original source, acquisition method, handlers, dates/times, hashes,
  storage, and transfer history for lawfully obtained digital artifacts.
- Preserve a source artifact separately from analytical derivatives where
  practical, and use current approved cryptographic methods appropriate to the
  retention purpose.

## What it does not establish

- A private legal preservation duty, admissibility, authenticity, authorship,
  version identity, or authority to collect data from a third party.
- That a cryptographic hash proves provenance or source-code completeness.

## Key facts and reasoning

The report emphasizes that digital files are readily altered and treats
documentation and integrity controls as complementary. That supports a
repeatable factual record, not an inference that a license condition was
triggered or breached.
