# AUTH-0110: U.S. copyright jurisdiction, venue, transfer, and supplemental claims

## Citation and source

- 28 U.S.C. §§ 1331, 1338(a)–(b), 1367, 1391, 1400(a), 1404(a), 1406(a), and 1631.
- [Official preliminary U.S. Code, Title 28, Part IV](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title28-part4); [§ 1391](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&req=%28title%3A28+section%3A1391+edition%3Aprelim%29); [§ 1400](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&req=%28title%3A28+section%3A1400+edition%3Aprelim%29).

## Procedural posture and weight

Operative federal statutes. They bind federal courts according to their terms,
as interpreted by controlling precedent. They do not resolve personal
jurisdiction, state-law claim elements, contractual forum selection, or a
foreign court's jurisdiction.

## Claims and requested relief

Not a pleading or relief source. Section 1338(a) gives federal district courts
original jurisdiction over civil actions arising under federal copyright law
and makes that jurisdiction exclusive for copyright claims. Section 1338(b)
addresses related unfair-competition claims; § 1367 supplies the general
supplemental-jurisdiction framework. Sections 1391 and 1400(a) govern venue;
§§ 1404(a), 1406(a), and 1631 address transfer or cure in specified settings.

## Holdings and relief actually awarded

No case holding or remedy is supplied by this card.

## What it supports

- A copyright complaint must separately analyze subject-matter jurisdiction,
  personal jurisdiction, venue, and any supplemental state-law claim.
- General venue and copyright-specific venue text must be checked together,
  along with controlling circuit authority and contract clauses.
- Transfer provisions can matter without deciding merits or validating a
  chosen forum.

## What it does not establish

- That a particular defendant is subject to personal jurisdiction, that venue
  is proper on a given fact record, or that a forum-selection clause controls.
- Governing law for a license or contract, discovery availability, or the
  recognition of a foreign judgment.

## Key facts and reasoning

Venue and jurisdiction are threshold procedural questions; the place of a
software repository, a web-accessible service, a downstream product sale, and
the parties' headquarters can each be relevant but are not substitutes for the
statutory and precedential tests.
