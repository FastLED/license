# AUTH-0093: Federal Rules of Civil Procedure 65(d) and 70

## Citation and source

- [Current Federal Rules PDF, Rules 65 and 70](https://www.uscourts.gov/file/78323/download) (U.S. Courts).
- Later history/current status: current PDF checked 2026-08-25; rule effective dates and local rules must be checked for the forum.

## Procedural posture and weight

Federal procedural rules apply in the federal court system as adopted and amended; local rules and case-specific orders can add requirements.

## Claims and requested relief

Rule 65(d) requires specificity and identifies persons bound by injunctions. Rule 70 supplies mechanisms after a judgment requires a party to perform a specific act. AUTH-0111 separately covers Rule 26(c) discovery protection.

## Holdings and relief actually awarded

Not a judicial decision. These rules do not establish an underlying copyright, contract, or source-disclosure entitlement.

## What it supports

- An injunction must say what it requires; contempt concerns a valid order and bound persons.
- Specific-act enforcement follows a judgment, rather than replacing merits proof.

## What it does not establish

- That source is discoverable in every case, that an order should issue, or that a third party is bound.

## Key facts and reasoning

The rules distinguish merits-injunction specificity from postjudgment performance enforcement. Rule 26(c) discovery protection is intentionally left to AUTH-0111 to avoid duplicating that authority.
