# PR-0020: Comparative and cross-border enforcement

> Preliminary research for attorney review. Not legal advice and not a license
> or policy decision.

## Scope and assumptions

This comparative routing memo synthesizes the bounded public-decision census in
[PR-0004](../case-census/international-open-source-license-enforcement-census.md).
It does not supply a country-by-country merits opinion, certified translation,
or worldwide case census. It assumes facts may connect more than one country:
author/contributor, code creation, repository/service operation, manufacture,
first sale, import, recipient, defendant domicile/assets, and alleged injury.
It separately identifies international baseline instruments, EU regional rules,
and country-specific primary-law sources. Original language controls whenever a
translation differs or a result would depend on wording.

## Short answer

The Berne Convention's national-treatment and independence-of-protection
framework and TRIPS enforcement obligations do not choose a court, determine
license assent, create a uniform source-release remedy, or guarantee cross-
border collection. The EU Brussels I Recast supplies regional civil-jurisdiction
rules for relevant defendants; the Enforcement Directive sets minimum EU
enforcement measures, subject to Member State implementation and substantive
law. France, Germany, the Netherlands, China, and South Korea each require
their own procedural/substantive-law, language, and current-status analysis.

The publicly accessible cases gathered in PR-0004 demonstrate that open-source
and reciprocal-license disputes have been litigated in multiple jurisdictions.
They do not establish a universal approach to covered-work scope, source
availability, standing, relief, or foreign judgment recognition. Use a primary
text in its source language and obtain qualified translation/local counsel
where it materially affects a conclusion.

## Analysis

### International instruments set floors, not a single litigation route

Article 5 of the [Berne Convention](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/283693)
addresses national treatment and independent protection for qualifying works.
TRIPS Articles 41 and 44 address enforcement procedures and injunctions in
member-state law. [WIPO's TRIPS text/status](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/treaties/details/231)
is a primary treaty resource. These instruments do not decide a private
contract's formation, which country's law applies, personal jurisdiction,
interim measures, evidence access, or judgment recognition in a concrete case.

### EU regional rules require member-state implementation and facts

For defendants domiciled in an EU Member State, [Brussels I Recast, Regulation
(EU) No 1215/2012](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A32012R1215)
contains the general domicile rule and special contract/tort jurisdiction rules.
It is not a global forum statute and does not determine substantive copyright or
contract merits. [Directive 2004/48/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=celex%3A32004L0048)
requires Member States to provide fair, equitable, effective, and proportionate
IP-enforcement measures; national implementation and domestic procedure decide
the available route. Neither text establishes that a specific license condition
is enforceable or that public source disclosure is mandatory.

### Country-specific primary law must not be generalized

| Jurisdiction | Primary material in this record | Permitted use / limit |
|---|---|---|
| France | [Intellectual Property Code](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/id/LEGITEXT000006069414/), *Entr'Ouvert* cards AUTH-0051–0053 | French sources/judgments; original French controls; remand/finality requires checking |
| Germany | [UrhG original](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/BJNR012730965.html), [official translation notice/text](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/englisch_urhg/index.html), AUTH-0054–0059 | German original controls; official translation warns it may lag; older case texts have stated source gaps |
| Netherlands | AUTH-0060, official ECLI identified on card | Read Dutch judgment and current Dutch procedure; English summaries are not controlling |
| China | AUTH-0061–0062 | Official summaries/reports are narrower than a full judgment; obtain full Chinese text/qualified translation |
| South Korea | AUTH-0063 | Korean Supreme Court record; qualified translation/local analysis needed for dispositive wording |
| United States | AUTH-0001–0006, AUTH-0030–0049, AUTH-0110–0114 | Federal/state split and selected forum remain material |

This table reports research access, not an assertion that the cited country has
the same rule or the only relevant law. A country-specific decision can be a
persuasive factual comparator but is not binding U.S. law; an untranslated
extract should not carry a dispositive proposition.

### Recognition and enforcement are a separate later stage

Within U.S. federal districts, 28 U.S.C. § 1963 can permit registration of
specified federal judgments; that does not govern a foreign judgment. The [2019
HCCH Judgments Convention](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/full-text/?cid=137+)
is a recognition/enforcement framework between Contracting States subject to its
scope, eligibility, exclusions, and status. Its [status table](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=137.)
shows the United States as a signatory without an entry-into-force date as of
the published table; do not assume it supplies a U.S. route. Outside an
applicable treaty, national law commonly controls recognition/enforcement and
can require a new proceeding. Interim injunctive relief, asset location,
service, appeal, public policy, and due process can all change practical value.

## Scenario matrix

| Scenario | First routing questions | Do not infer |
|---|---|---|
| EU seller distributes in several Member States | Defendant domicile, forum rule, territorial acts, applicable law | One Member State's case determines all others |
| U.S. owner targets foreign manufacturer | Defendant/assets, local IP law, service, judgment destination | U.S. order automatically operates abroad |
| French/German case is cited | Original text, translation, posture, later history, exact license | English summary supplies a universal holding |
| Chinese/Korean public report exists | Full judgment availability, parties, appeal, qualified translation | Summary proves every pleaded fact/remedy |
| Foreign money/order judgment sought in U.S. | Origin jurisdiction, finality, applicable recognition regime/state law | § 1963 or HCCH automatically applies |

## Research checklist for counsel review

- Map every territorial act, party domicile/nationality, asset, product/service,
  work, contract/license version, and proposed enforcement destination.
- Identify the precise forum rule, substantive law, conflicts rule, service
  route, limitations/registration rule, evidence access, interim-relief rule,
  and recognition regime in original/current primary sources.
- Preserve original-language court materials; record source, docket/ECLI,
  procedural posture, finality, and translation provenance. Use qualified
  translation/local counsel when wording is material.
- Treat treaty/recast/directive language as its own level of authority; then
  identify national implementing and procedural law.
- Examine judgment enforcement separately from merits, including assets,
  finality, appeal, notice/due process, public policy, and treaty status.

## What would change the answer

- Selected countries/forums, exact terms, defendant domicile/assets, actual
  territorial conduct, a certified translation, final judgment, or changed
  treaty status.

## Open questions for counsel

- Which markets and forums are material enough for country-specific advice?
- Whether any contract selects law/forum and whether it reaches each party/claim.
- Whether certified/qualified translation is needed for a dispositive authority.
- Whether a target state recognizes a proposed foreign judgment and on what terms.

## Repository implications

This memo makes no country-specific license recommendation and proposes no
enforcement action or `LICENSE` revision. It preserves the attorney/local-
counsel review gate for material cross-border questions.
